The exercise — the pilot

We will conduct a small exercise. Imagine you are a pilot and you crash-landed on a deserted island. Through a spark of ingenuity showcasing a superhuman capability you manage to fix the plane. In an even more baffling feat you harvest the appropriate fuel and prepare for takeoff. You fly away by the skin of your teeth, just to crash-land again.

No matter the ingenuity and impossible chemistry knowledge you demonstrated, your license would be revoked. A pilot cannot go around crash-landing and still fly, and there's no reasonable way one pilot could manage doing the work that requires two. Instead of trying to force repairs on a broken plane, we should take inventory and see what caused the malfunctions in the first place. This will allow you to put up appropriate barriers and safety devices for the next time you travel.

— ✈ —

Chances are you are not a bad pilot. You probably go above and beyond, work extra hard and see to your duties well. However, before you make any drastic decisions, you need to heal. If your plane crash-landed and caused you to break some bones you would need to see to them before you could safely utilize your limbs. After betrayal, real quantifiable damage has occurred and you need the time, space, and healing powers from the universe before you can navigate again.

You are a wonderful mix of biology and stardust. You have complicated and intriguing parts of the universe inside you. Someday someone will look at you and wonder how they have gotten so lucky. Don't stand in the way of progress. Get rid of the problem and wait for someone worth it, it's worth it.
