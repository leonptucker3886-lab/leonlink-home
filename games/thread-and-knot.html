1. The Premise

You are a weaver-oracle. A querent sits before you with a question. You do not shuffle a tarot deck and lay out archetypes — instead you cast a web: six threads drawn across a circle, each one a current in the querent's life. Where threads cross, a knot forms, and every knot speaks.

At the end, you must cut one thread. The cut releases the reading and fixes the verdict. What you cut — and what you spare — is the whole art.

This is a game for one person. You may ask a real question about your own life, or a fictional one about a character in a story you are writing. Both work identically.

2. Materials

  • A pen or pencil.
  • One sheet of paper — plain, unlined, large enough for a circle roughly the size of your open hand.
  • One standard deck of 52 cards, jokers removed.
  • A question. Real or invented.

Card values: Ace = 1, 2–10 = face value, Jack = 11, Queen = 12, King = 13. (This differs from most games — in the web, the King is the highest rank.)

3. The Question

Write your question at the top of the page. Phrase it as a question you genuinely do not know the answer to. Good questions are specific enough to matter and open enough to be answered by a web:

  • Will I find work before winter?
  • Should I trust the offer from the stranger?
  • What is my brother hiding from me?
  • Will the harvest hold?

Write the question in ink. It stays on the page; the reading is for it and it alone.

The Stake — Before casting, the querent writes down one suit they hope the verdict will be, and keeps it hidden until the cut. Cut the staked suit: +1 Reputation. Cut any other suit: −1 Reputation. If the staked suit has no thread in the web, the Stake is unmet: −1 Reputation.

4. The Loom

Draw a large circle on the page. Mark 13 dots around its rim, numbered clockwise from the top:

13 stations around the rim 1 13 2 12 3 11 4 10 5 9 6 8 7 1 = top. Numbers run clockwise. 13 stations, evenly spaced.

The thirteen stations

StationName
1The Door
2The Road
3The Hearth
4The Market
5The Field
6The River
7The Tower
8The Wood
9The Well
10The Chapel
11The Grave
12The Star
13The Crown

These are the stations. Every thread will begin and end at one of them.

Why thirteen?

Thirteen is the number of ranks in a suit, so every card can be placed without ambiguity. It is also, traditionally, an uncomfortable number — which suits a fortune-telling game.

5. Casting the Web

Shuffle the deck. Draw six cards one at a time. For each card:

  1. The rank gives one station. Ace = station 1, 2–10 = that station, Jack = 11, Queen = 12, King = 13.
  2. The suit gives the other station:
    SuitStation
    ♠1
    ♥4
    ♦7
    ♣10
  3. Draw a straight line — a thread — between those two stations. Write the card's suit and rank small beside the thread.
  4. If both stations are the same (for example, the Ace of Spades is rank 1 and suit 1), do not draw a line. Instead draw a small closed loop at that station. A loop is a special omen — see Knots.

After all six threads are drawn, mark every point where two threads cross with a small circle. That circle is a knot. Do not count crossings at the rim (threads meeting at a shared station) — only interior crossings count.

If two cards draw the exact same thread (for example 4♥ and 4♠ both join stations 1 and 4), they count as a single thread. No knot forms between them.

A note on drawing

Threads should be straight. If three threads all cross at the same point, that is one knot involving three threads — read the dominant pair (see Knots) and note the third as an intensifier.

6. Reading the Threads

Each thread is a current in the querent's life. Read all six, in the order you drew them. The suit is the nature of the current; the rank is its strength.

The four natures

SuitCurrentDomain
♠IronConflict, obstacle, strength, endurance
♥BloodLove, connection, feeling, kinship
♦GoldFortune, work, value, exchange
♣RootKnowledge, growth, hidden things, patience

The thirteen strengths

RankStrength
2–4A faint current — barely felt, easily missed
5–7A steady current — present and reliable
8–10A strong current — impossible to ignore
J (11)A current that acts — a person or event that moves things
Q (12)A current that receives — an influence, a listener, a vessel
K (13)A current that rules — a dominant force, hard to resist
A (1)A current at a threshold — a beginning or an ending

Write one sentence for each thread. For example: "A steady current of gold — money is present but not abundant." Or: "A ruling current of iron — a conflict dominates the querent's life, and they cannot set it down."

Read a thread by its endpoints too — Iron running from The Field to The Door is a different story from Iron running from The Grave to The Crown.

7. Reading the Knots

Each knot is a moment where two currents meet. The two suits of the crossing threads give the omen. Read each knot in turn, and note its distance from the center of the loom:

  • Near the center — the omen is immediate, close at hand.
  • Near the rim — the omen is distant, approaching from far away.
PairOmen
♠Iron on iron — a grinding conflict, strength against strength. Two forces that will not yield.
♥Blood on blood — love that deepens, or love that wounds. Kinship tested.
♦Gold on gold — fortune compounding, or greed that feeds itself.
♣Root on root — knowledge that grows, or a hidden tangle that will not be pulled loose.
♠♥Iron and blood — a love that must fight, or conflict inside the heart.
♠♦Iron and gold — money at the point of a sword. Gain that comes at a cost.
♠♣Iron and root — a lesson that hurts, or knowledge turned into a weapon.
♥♦Blood and gold — love and money intertwined. A bond with a price.
♥♣Blood and root — love that teaches, or the roots of family.
♦♣Gold and root — wealth from knowledge, or knowledge that must be bought.

Loops

If a thread is a loop — a card whose rank station and suit station coincide — it has no knots of its own, but it colors every knot it touches. Read a loop as a closed circuit:

♠A conflict with no exit — a fight that feeds itself.
♥A love turned inward — self-regard, or grief that will not release.
♦A fortune that circles back — money gained and lost to the same cause.
♣Knowledge that feeds itself — a study with no application, or a secret kept for its own sake.

A loop is never cut (see The Cutting) — it is already closed. If you draw two loops, the reading is doubly closed: the querent is caught in a cycle, and no single cut will free them.

In a normal reading with a single loop, the loop simply colors the knots and cannot be cut — cut another thread. The Loop Reading variant below overrides this: if any loop appears, the verdict is read from the loop instead of a cut thread.

8. The Cutting

Now — and only now, after all threads and knots have been read — choose one thread to cut. Erase it. Every knot that lay on that thread dissolves: those moments are averted. The knots that remain are what comes true.

The card of the cut thread becomes the Verdict — the final answer to the question. Read it three ways:

The nature of the outcome — by suit

SuitOutcome
♠A hard truth. The outcome is difficult but strengthening. You will be glad of it later.
♥A favorable outcome in matters of the heart. Warmth, connection, repair.
♦Material gain or loss. Coin, work, exchange — the outcome is tangible.
♣A slow, hidden, enduring outcome. It will come quietly and last a long time.

The timing — by rank

RankWhen
AceNow. Immediately, or already underway.
2–5Within days.
6–10Within weeks.
J, Q, KWithin months.

The clarity — by knots on the cut thread

Count the knots that lay on the thread you cut. That number determines how clearly the verdict lands — see Clarity below.

The decision

You read the whole web before you cut. So by the time you choose, you know which threads carry which knots, and which verdicts they would produce. The art is in choosing the thread that gives the querent the reading they need — not necessarily the one they want.

9. Clarity

The number of knots on the cut thread determines how clearly the verdict lands. This is the game's only scoring — and it is not a win or a loss, but a quality of reading.

Knots on the cut threadClarityWhat it means
0–1 Thin The reading is faint. The querent doubts you, or forgets the verdict by morning. The verdict is half-true — it comes to pass, but not in the way you said.
2–3 Clear The reading lands. The querent believes it, remembers it, and it comes true as spoken. This is the reading a fortune teller hopes for.
4+ Tangled The reading overwhelms. Too many currents meet on one thread; the querent is frightened or confused. The verdict is true but costly — it comes to pass, and something is lost in the passing.

Reputation

Track Reputation in every reading, starting at 3. After each reading:

Clear reading, favorable verdict (♥ or ♦)+2
Clear reading, hard verdict (♠ or ♣)+1
Thin reading−1
Tangled reading−1 (but +1 if the verdict is favorable — the querent is impressed and shaken)
Cutting the staked suit+1
Cutting any other suit−1

At Reputation 0, your fortune teller is discredited — no one comes to the loom, and the campaign ends. At Reputation 10, they are renowned — the reading is sought by the powerful, and the final reading is a royal question: not "what will happen," but "what will you do about it." After that reading, the campaign ends.

10. The Follow-Up

After the reading, you may ask one follow-up question — a clarification, a consequence, a "but what about—"

Do not cast a new web. Read the remaining threads — the five you did not cut — as the answer. The follow-up is answered by what was spared, not by what was released. This is the oldest rule of the art: the answer to the second question is always in the first reading, and you simply did not see it.

11. A Sample Reading

The Question — a worked example

"Will I find work before winter?" — asked by a querent named Maren, a cartwright whose workshop burned in the spring.

The cast. Six cards are drawn: 7♥, K♠, 3♦, Q♣, 9♠, 5♣. Six threads are drawn between their stations. Three knots form — all three on the 5♣ thread:

  • The 5♣ thread crosses the 7♥ thread.
  • The 5♣ thread crosses the 3♦ thread.
  • The 5♣ thread crosses the 9♠ thread.

Reading the threads.

  • 7♥ — a steady current of blood: Maren's family is present and reliable.
  • K♠ — a ruling current of iron: a dominant conflict — the loss of the workshop — governs her days.
  • 3♦ — a faint current of gold: money is scarce, barely felt.
  • Q♣ — a receiving current of root: knowledge is being offered to her — someone is teaching her something.
  • 9♠ — a strong current of iron: a second conflict, nearly as strong as the first.
  • 5♣ — a steady current of root: knowledge is growing in her, quietly, the way a seed grows before it breaks the soil.

Reading the knots.

  • ♥+♣ (blood and root) — love that teaches, or the roots of family. Near the rim: distant.
  • ♦+♣ (gold and root) — wealth from knowledge, or knowledge that must be bought. Mid-radius.
  • ♠+♣ (iron and root) — a lesson that hurts, or knowledge turned into a weapon. Mid-radius.

The cutting. Maren needs work, so the reading must land on gold. Look at the threads:

  • Cut 7♥: 1 knot → Thin. Verdict: ♥, rank 7 → favorable, within weeks. But blood, not gold — the answer is about family, not work.
  • Cut K♠: 0 knots → Thin. Verdict: ♠, King → a hard truth, within months. Too slow, too faint.
  • Cut 3♦: 1 knot → Thin. Verdict: ♦, rank 3 → material gain, within days. This is the reading Maren needs — but it lands Thin.
  • Cut Q♣: 0 knots → Thin. Verdict: ♣, Queen → hidden outcome, within months. Too vague.
  • Cut 9♠: 1 knot → Thin. Verdict: ♠, rank 9 → a hard truth, within weeks. Work comes, but it costs.
  • Cut 5♣: 3 knots → Clear. Verdict: ♣, rank 5 → a slow, hidden, enduring outcome, within days. The clearest thread in the web — but it speaks of hidden knowledge, not work.

The reading given. The fortune teller cuts the 3♦ thread. The verdict is ♦ at rank 3, Thin: "Work will come within days, and it will be paid work — but it will be small, and it will not be the workshop. Take it anyway. The gold is faint now, but it is real." The clearest thread in the web was the 5♣ — a Clear reading, but of hidden knowledge, not of work. A good oracle gives the reading the querent needs, not the clearest one on the loom.

The knot on the 3♦ thread — the gold-and-root knot — dissolves. It is averted. What remains is the blood-and-root knot and the iron-and-root knot: the family that teaches, and the hard lesson. Those come true. Maren will find work, and her family will still be at the center of it, and she will still have to learn what the fire taught her.

12. Variants

VariantRule
Oracle and Querent — two players One player is the Oracle: they cast, read, and cut. The other is the Querent: they ask the question, secretly write a Stake (the suit they hope the verdict will be) before the cast, and judge the reading at the end — they say whether it landed Thin, Clear, or Tangled, by how true it feels. The Querent's judgment is the score, and the Oracle's Reputation moves by it. Swap roles for the next question.
Face-Down Cast Draw all six cards face-down and draw the threads before revealing any suits. The geometry is fixed first; the meanings arrive afterward. This produces stranger, less legible webs — good for questions you are afraid of.
Double Cast Cast two webs for the same question. The thread whose suit appears in both webs is the true thread — cut that one, and the verdict is certain. If no suit repeats, the question is unanswerable tonight.
The Querent's Choice Instead of choosing the cut yourself, draw one card after the reading. The suit of that card determines which thread is cut (if two threads share that suit, cut the one with more knots). The querent, not the oracle, decides.
The Loop Reading If any card is a loop (rank station = suit station), the reading is closed. You may not cut a loop, and the verdict must be read from the loop's suit and rank instead of a cut thread. Closed readings are rare and always significant.
Three Questions Ask three questions before casting. Cast one web. Read the same web three times, once for each question — but you may cut only one thread in total, and the cut answers only one of the three. The other two are answered by the remaining threads, and are always Thin.
The Skeptic A hard mode. After the reading, draw one more card. If it is black, the querent does not believe you; the verdict is true but will not be acted upon. If red, they believe. Face cards are ambiguous — the querent believes, but tells no one.

A closing note

This game is not a test of prediction. It is a way of thinking sideways about a question — of letting a drawn shape suggest what you already half-knew. The verdict is not fate; it is a sentence you wrote, and then agreed to hear. Play it as an art, not an oracle, and it will reward you for years.
