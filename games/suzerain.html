1. The Premise

You are an upstart warlord in a broken land. Four great powers hold the continent between them. Every card you draw is a card your enemies cannot draw. Every card you hoard is a weapon withheld from fate.

You play alone, against the deck. A campaign runs 60–120 minutes and can be paused at any point — just note which phase you stopped in.

2. Components

  • One standard deck of 52 cards, jokers removed.
  • Two sheets of paper: a map sheet and a ledger sheet.
  • A pen or pencil.

Card values

Ace = 1 or 11 (choose when played). 2–10 = face value. Jack, Queen, King = 10.

Troops are a single army recorded as one number in your ledger — there are no per-province garrisons.

The four rivals

Name them yourself and write the names in your ledger. The suits are the engine; the flavor is yours.

SuitDefault flavor
♠The Iron Legion — militarists
♥The Sun Court — zealots and nobles
♦The Merchant League — coin and contracts
♣The Wild Clans — raiders and hunters

3. Setup

Step 1 — Build the world

Draw 20 cards one at a time. Each card creates one province. Record it in the ledger and draw it on your map as one box of a 5×4 grid. Only orthogonal neighbours are adjacent.

Setup cardProvince ownerDefenseGold value
Rank 2–6Neutral (independent)31
Rank 7–10The card's suit42
J · Q · KThe card's suit52
AceThe card's suit63

ExampleYou draw 8♦ — a Merchant League province, Defense 4, worth 2 Gold. You draw 4♠ — a Neutral province, Defense 3, worth 1 Gold. On a 2–6, the suit is ignored; the ♠ only means “independent.”

Name each province as you place it. Names make the map feel real and make your log readable.

Step 2 — Choose your home

Pick any Neutral province. That is your capital. Record in the ledger:

Gold2
Troops3
Hand0
Threat1

Step 3 — Deal fate

Shuffle the remaining 32 cards. Set aside 12 of them face-down — these are the Sealed Fates, and they take no part in this campaign. The other 20 cards are the Fate Deck. It is your resource and your enemy.

Step 4 — Record everything

Your hand, gold, troops, and province states live on paper. Cards in hand stay face-up in front of you — but write them down too, so the game can be rebuilt exactly if they're disturbed.

4. The Map Sheet

Draw a 5×4 grid. In each box write:

  • Province name (small, in a corner)
  • Owner initial: Neutral, ♠♥♦♣, or You
  • Defense number (circle it if fortified)
  • Gold value

Use pencil. This map will be redrawn many times.

5. The Turn — one Season

I. Dawn

  1. Collect Gold: gain Gold equal to the total value of provinces you own.
  2. Draw to hand: draw until your hand reaches hand size (start: 5). If the Fate Deck runs out mid-draw, see The Reckoning.

II. Day — take up to 3 actions

Each action costs 1 card from hand unless noted. You may take the same action more than once, but no more than 3 actions in a Day. Spending Gold (below) is free and does not count toward the 3.

The spent pile

Cards you play, spend, or discard go to a spent pile and leave the game — they are never reshuffled. Only cards flipped at Night go to the discard pile, which is what the Reckoning reshuffles.

ActionCostEffect
Conquer1+ cardsAttack an adjacent province. See Combat.
Conscript1 cardGain Troops equal to half the card's value, rounded down, min 1 (face cards = 5).
Fortify1 card+1 permanent Defense to one province you own (max 8).
Settle1 card+1 Gold value to one province you own (max 3).
Treaty1 cardName a rival. The next time that rival would activate at Night, it does not — whenever that happens. The card must match the rival's suit.
Rally2 cardsDiscard both, draw 2. Hand filtering.

Spend Gold at any time during Day:

  • 1 Gold → 1 Troop (max 3 per Season)
  • 2 Gold → draw 1 card
  • 3 Gold → +1 permanent Defense to a province you own

III. Night — the world moves

Flip cards from the Fate Deck equal to your current Threat (starts at 1). Resolve each flip separately:

  1. The suit tells you which rival acts. Ignore the rank for choosing.
  2. Find that rival's target: the province nearest to any territory they own that they do not own. Use shortest path across the grid. If two provinces are equally near, the rival targets the one with the lowest Defense; if still tied, you choose.
  3. Resolve the attack using the flipped card's value plus your current Threat as their force (A = 11 — so a Queen at Threat 2 attacks with force 12):
    TargetResult
    NeutralThey take it if force ≥ its Defense. Otherwise nothing happens.
    YoursYou may defend by playing cards from hand and/or spending Troops (1 Troop = +2 Defense), adding to the province's Defense. If your total ≥ their force, the attack is repelled and you keep the province. If not, the province falls to that rival — remove your marker.
    Another rival'sThey take it if force ≥ Defense + 2.
  4. If a rival owns no provinces at all, the flip becomes a Raid: you lose 1 Troop and 1 Gold. If you have no Troops and no Gold, the nearest province you own loses 1 Defense permanently.

Cards flipped at Night go to a discard pile, face up. They are not available to you.

6. Combat — Conquer

Choose an adjacent province you own to launch the attack from, and an adjacent province you don't own as the target. Play cards from hand. Your total must equal or exceed the target's Defense. Add +2 to its Defense if the province belongs to its own suit — patriots resist. Neutral provinces get no bonus.

You may also spend Troops during an attack: 1 Troop = +2 to your total, declared before comparing. Troops spent for an attack boost are lost.

Your total vs. DefenseOutcome
Total ≥ Defense + 5Province taken. No losses.
Total ≥ Defense + 1Province taken. Lose 1 Troop.
Total = Defense exactlyProvince taken. Lose 2 Troops (pyrrhic).
Total < DefenseAttack fails. Lose 2 Troops and all cards played.

Captured provinces keep their Defense and Gold value. Enemy provinces you take are now simply yours — no bonus, no penalty. If an outcome would make you lose more Troops than you have, you lose what you have — Troops never go below 0.

Champions — When you conquer a province by playing a King as part of your attack total, name a Champion in your ledger. A Champion grants +2 to any Conquer total launched from a province adjacent to the Champion's province. Each province can hold one Champion; if the province falls to a rival, the Champion is lost.

7. The Reckoning

When the Fate Deck is exhausted:

  1. Shuffle the discard pile into a new Fate Deck.
  2. Threat increases by 1 (maximum 4). More cards flip at Night.
  3. If you reshuffle while Threat is already 4, the campaign ends — score your holdings.

If the discard pile is empty when the Fate Deck runs out, the campaign ends — score your holdings.

Entrenchment — At each Reckoning, before the new Fate Deck is shuffled, each rival adds +1 Defense to one of its border provinces (the one nearest your territory; maximum 8).

This is the clock

Every card you spend is a card that never reaches the discard pile to be reshuffled. Hoarding cards slows the Reckoning but starves your expansion. That tension is the whole game.

8. Winning & Losing

VictoryControl 14 of 20 provinces, or reduce all four rivals to zero territory.
DefeatLose your last province, or any single rival controls 10+ provinces.
Endgame scoreIf the Reckoning ends the campaign: 1 point per province, 2 per fortified province, 1 per 5 Gold, 1 per 3 Troops. Record it as your dynasty's legacy.

10. Sample Season

Season One — Threat 1

You hold 5 cards, 4 Gold, 6 Troops, and 5 provinces. The ♥ Sun Court sits two provinces from your border.

Dawn: Collect 7 Gold (total 11). Draw to 5 cards.

Day: You have 3 actions. You want the Neutral province of Greyford (Defense 3, value 1) on your flank. You play a 4♣ — total 4, beating Defense 3 by 1, so you take it and lose 1 Troop. You play a 9♦ on Conscript: gain 4 Troops. You spend 3 Gold to Fortify your capital to Defense 4. You keep three cards in hand for defense.

Night: Threat is 1, so flip one card: Q♥. The Sun Court attacks. Their nearest target is your new province, Greyford (Defense 3). Force 11 (Queen 10 + Threat 1). You play your held 6♠ and spend 1 Troop (+2) — total 3 + 6 + 2 = 11. Repelled. You keep Greyford, but your hand is down to two cards.

Lesson: Greyford cost you three cards and a Troop, and the Q♥ nearly took it anyway. Next Season, consider a Treaty with ♥ before you expand toward the Sun Court.

11. Variants

VariantRule
Hard modeThreat starts at 2.
Slow burnRivals only act on a flip if the card's rank is 6 or higher. Longer, more builder-friendly campaign.
Rival warWhen two rivals fight, the loser's card is set aside. The rival with the most set-aside cards is the Ascendant and flips twice at Night.
Dynasty modeWhen a campaign ends, note your final province count. Next campaign, start with Troops equal to half that number (max 6). Your previous empire's ruins enrich the new world.
