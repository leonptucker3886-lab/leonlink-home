Before You Begin

Every game here is solitaire: no second player, no game master. Each uses one standard 52-card deck (jokers removed) and a sheet of paper for tracking. A few ask for a pencil-drop or a deliberate "look away" moment to generate hidden information — that is the only unusual thing you will ever be asked to do.

Unless a game says otherwise, rank values are: Ace = 11, Jack/Queen/King = 10, number cards at face value. Each game states its own exceptions.

How to use this page

Pick one game and play it to completion before reading the next. Each is a self-contained system; the fun is in learning its pressure points. Print the page (Ctrl/Cmd+P) for a clean paper copy — the dark theme strips out automatically.

01The Lantern Keepers

Survival horror

Time: 45–75 minTrack: Oil · Wick · Nerve · NightDifficulty: Medium

You are the keeper of a lighthouse on a bare rock. Relief comes in thirteen nights. Until then, the lamp must burn — or the ships will break on the reef, and their crews will come for you.

Setup

Shuffle the deck. On your ledger write:

TrackStartMeaning
Oil16Fuel for the lamp. Spent every night.
Wick4The lamp's integrity. At 0, the light dies.
Nerve10Your mind. At 0, you abandon the light.
Night1Thirteen nights to survive.

Draw 5 cards into your hand. These are your Stores — preparations, spare parts, and courage. Maximum hand size is 5.

If the deck runs out, shuffle the discard pile into a new deck.

Each Night

  1. The Sky. Draw 3 cards face-up, left to right. These are tonight's events.
  2. The Watch. Resolve each card in order (see table below).
  3. The Light. Spend Oil equal to 1 + floor((Night − 1) ÷ 4). If you cannot, the light fails — you lose.
  4. The Wick. On nights 3, 6, 9, and 12, Wick −1. If Wick reaches 0, you lose.
  5. The Stores. Draw back up to 5 cards.

Event resolution

SuitEventEffect
♠Storm Lose Oil equal to ceil(rank ÷ 3). Cancel by discarding a Store card whose rank is ≥ this card.
♥Visitor Choose Shelter or Bar. Shelter: draw 1 card — red means they are trustworthy (+2 Nerve); black means they are a threat (−2 Nerve, −1 Oil). Bar: −1 Nerve.
♦Supply Gain 3 Oil, or draw 1 card.
♣Omen Lose 1 Nerve (2 if the card is a face card).

Crisis

If any of the three event cards is a face card (J, Q, K), draw a fourth card immediately and resolve it after the others. A night with a face card is always a long night.

Between nights

You may take any of these actions during the day, as often as you can afford:

  • Repair Wick: spend 3 Oil → Wick +1 (max 6).
  • Steady yourself: spend 1 card from Stores → Nerve +1 (max 10).
  • Scavenge: discard 2 cards → draw 2.

Winning & Losing

VictorySurvive all 13 nights with the lamp still burning.
DefeatOil insufficient at The Light, Wick reaches 0, or Nerve reaches 0.
ScoreOil + Wick + Nerve remaining.
The pressure

Oil is the obvious resource, but Nerve is the hidden one. Sheltering visitors is the only reliable way to gain Nerve — and it is a coin flip. A keeper who turns everyone away dies sane and alone.

02The Auction of Small Gods

Bidding & economics

Time: 30–45 minTrack: Coins · Lots won · Rival pursesDifficulty: Medium

Five rounds. Twenty lots. Three rivals with deeper pockets and stranger tastes than yours. You are a collector of minor deities, and tonight the auction house will decide who among you is a connoisseur and who is merely rich.

Setup

Shuffle the deck. On your ledger write:

  • Your Coins: 40
  • The Widow's Purse: 55 — she bids high on ♥ and ♦ (red suits).
  • The Colonel's Purse: 55 — he bids high on ♠ and ♣ (black suits).
  • The Child's Purse: 45 — she bids high on face cards and aces.

Draw a 5×4 grid on your paper. That is the auction floor. You will record each lot you win in it.

The Rounds

Play 5 rounds. Each round, reveal 4 lots (draw 4 cards face-up). Resolve them one at a time, in the order drawn.

The Catalog. Once per round, before bidding on the current lot, you may peek at the next lot in the deck.

If the deck runs out, shuffle the discard pile into a new deck.

Bidding on a lot

  1. Each rival flips one card from the deck. Their bid is that card's rank value plus a modifier:
RivalModifier
The Widow+3 if the lot is ♥ or ♦; −2 if ♠ or ♣.
The Colonel+3 if the lot is ♠ or ♣; −2 if ♥ or ♦.
The Child+4 if the lot is a face card or ace; −2 otherwise.
  1. A rival bids 0 (passes) if the result is below 1, or if their Purse cannot cover the bid.
  2. You bid any number of Coins, minimum 1. You may pass.
  3. Highest bid wins. Ties go to you. The winner pays their bid — you to the bank, the rival from their Purse.
  4. Record the lot in your grid, or note it under the rival's tally.

Scoring

After Round 5, score your collection:

HoldingPoints
Each lot wonIts rank value (A = 14, K = 13, Q = 12, J = 11)
Set of 3+ of the same suit+10
Pair of the same rank+5 per pair
Exactly one 9+5 (the Nine of Cups is the collector's lucky card)

Each rival scores the same way, but counts only lots they won. Compare your total to the highest-scoring rival.

VictoryYour score exceeds the highest rival's score.
DefeatAny rival outscores you, or you end with 0 Coins and fewer than 3 lots.
ScoreYour total minus the highest rival's total. Positive is a win; record the margin.
The pressure

You cannot win every lot. The Widow will outbid you on hearts, the Colonel on spades, and the Child will steal any ace you truly want. Your job is to find the cheap lots they ignore — the off-suit cards that still complete your sets — and to know when a lot is a trap.

03The Machine

Optimization puzzle

Time: 20–40 minTrack: Flow · Target · Slots · TurnsDifficulty: Hard (mathy)

You are an inventor with a workbench, a blueprint, and a deadline. Assemble four components into a working machine that produces exactly the output the patron demands. Miss the target and the patron walks — and takes the funding with him.

Setup

Rank values in The Machine: Jack = 11, Queen = 12, King = 13, Ace = 11. (This is the one game in the Cabinet that uses these values — everywhere else, face cards are 10.)

  1. Draw the Target: flip one card. Target = its rank value × 6. (A 7 → 42. A King → 78. An Ace → 66.) Write it down.
  2. Start Flow: 12. This is the output of your boiler before any components.
  3. Workbench: deal 8 cards face-up. These are your available components.
  4. Slots: draw 4 empty boxes in a row on your paper. Components are installed left to right.
  5. Turns: you have 8 turns.

The Components

Each card is a component. Its suit is its operation; its rank is its value.

SuitComponentOperation on Flow
♠GearFlow + rank
♥SpringFlow × (rank ÷ 4, rounded down, min 1) — so 4–7 = ×1, 8–11 = ×2, 12–13 = ×3
♦ConduitFlow − rank
♣ValveFlow ÷ (rank ÷ 4, rounded up, min 1) — so 2–4 = ÷1, 5–8 = ÷2, 9–12 = ÷3, 13 = ÷4

Operations apply in slot order, left to right. Round down after every division, and treat any negative flow as 0.

Each Turn

Choose one:

  • Install: take a card from the Workbench and place it in the next empty slot. Once installed, it is locked — it cannot be moved or swapped.
  • Scrap: discard up to 2 cards from the Workbench and redraw to 8.

You may also spend a turn doing nothing, if you prefer to keep the Workbench intact.

The Spare Part. Once per campaign, hold one component in reserve: a fifth card kept aside. Before resolution, you may swap it into any one slot, discarding the component it replaces.

Resolution

After all four slots are filled, calculate the final Flow through the machine. Compare it to the Target:

ResultOutcome
Final Flow exactly equals TargetMasterwork. Score 3.
Within ±3 of TargetSuccess. Score 2.
Within ±8 of TargetPartial. Score 1.
Further than 8 awayFailure. Score 0.

If you fill all four slots before using all 8 turns, you may not undo. The machine is built; you live with it.

If 8 turns end with unfilled slots, the machine is incomplete: score 0 for that machine.

Winning & Losing

There is no opponent — only the target and your own arithmetic. A campaign is three machines in a row. Add your scores:

7–9Master inventor. Your name enters the guild ledger.
4–6Respectable. The patron pays, but grumbles.
1–3You keep the workshop, barely.
0Ruined. The patron takes the building.
The pressure

Because components lock once installed, the game is about sequencing, not just arithmetic. A ×3 Spring placed early multiplies everything after it; the same Spring placed late multiplies almost nothing. Scrapping is your only undo — and it costs a turn you may not have.

04The Regent's Ledger

Political management

Time: 40–60 minTrack: 4 Loyalties · Treasury · Month · HandDifficulty: Medium–Hard

You govern in the name of a child king. For twelve months you must hold four factions together, spend a treasury that is never quite enough, and survive until the boy comes of age. The kingdom does not care that you mean well.

Setup

Shuffle the deck. On your ledger write:

FactionSuitLoyalty
The Crown (the boy king's court)♠7
The Church♥7
The Guilds♦7
The People♣7

Treasury: 20 Gold. Month: 1. Hand: 0 cards (max 3).

Each Month

  1. Income. Gain 5 Gold, minus 1 for each faction below Loyalty 3.
  2. The Council. Draw 2 cards face-up. Each card is an issue from the faction of its suit — two issues may hit the same faction in one month.
  3. Resolve each issue — choose one:
ChoiceCost / Effect
Pay it offSpend Gold equal to ceil(rank ÷ 2). Loyalty unchanged.
AppeaseDiscard a hand card of the same suit. Loyalty unchanged, no Gold spent.
Ignore itLoyalty −1 (−2 if the card is a face card).
  1. The Petition. Draw 1 card into your hand (max 3). Then choose: Grant it — gain 1 Loyalty with that suit's faction, spend 2 Gold — or Refuse — that faction loses 1 Loyalty.
  2. Advance the month.

Special Months

WhenEvent
Months 4, 8, 12Crisis. Draw 1 extra card. All four factions lose 1 Loyalty unless you spend 5 Gold.
Month 6The King's Demand. Draw a card. Grant it (spend 3 Gold, Crown +1) or refuse (Crown −2).
Month 12Coming of Age. If the Crown's Loyalty is below 6, the king purges you — you lose, regardless of anything else.

A Marriage. Once per game, spend 8 Gold to marry the Crown to another faction: Crown +2 Loyalty and that faction +1 Loyalty.

Winning & Losing

VictorySurvive 12 months with all four factions above 0 and Crown Loyalty at least 6.
DefeatAny faction reaches 0 (you are deposed), or the Month 12 check fails.
ScoreTotal final Loyalty + (Treasury ÷ 2, rounded down).
The pressure

You cannot pay every issue. The treasury is deliberately too small. The skill is in choosing which faction to let grumble — and remembering that the Crown is the one that can end you at Month 12, no matter how beloved you are elsewhere.

05The Cartographer's Lie

Exploration & risk

Time: 30–50 minTrack: Provisions · Position · Map gridDifficulty: Medium

You bought a map of an island from a man who smiled too much. The treasure is real — you have seen the coin he was paid with. But some of the landmarks on his map are lies, and every false landmark costs you time you cannot spare.

Setup

  1. Build the island. Deal 36 cards face-down in a 6×6 grid. Do not look at them. This is the island.
  2. Set aside the remaining 16 cards, face-down, out of play.
  3. Mark your Start at the bottom-left cell and the Treasure at the top-right cell.
  4. Write Provisions: 20 on your ledger.

The Rule of the Map

A cell is a false landmark if its card is a ♠ of rank 2–7. There are exactly six such cards in the deck. Everything else is real terrain.

Each Turn

Choose one:

ActionCostEffect
Move 1 Provision Step to an orthogonally adjacent cell and flip its card. If it is a false landmark (♠ 2–7), lose an additional 3 Provisions — you have wasted time at a place that does not exist.
Probe 1 Provision Flip an adjacent cell's card without moving into it. You learn whether it is safe, but you do not travel there. The card stays face-up.
Retrace 2 Provisions Step onto a cell you have already visited. (Without this, a wrong turn can strand you with no legal move.)
Study the Map 1 Provision Look at any one face-down card anywhere on the island, then turn it face-down again.

You may never move diagonally. You may not move onto a cell you have already visited. A false landmark you have already flipped costs nothing more to step on (the danger is spent).

Winning & Losing

VictoryReach the Treasure cell with Provisions above 0.
DefeatProvisions reach 0 before you reach the Treasure. You are stranded.
ScoreProvisions remaining × 10, plus +5 for each false landmark you correctly probed before stepping on it.

Optional Deduction Layer

If you want a puzzle rather than a gamble, add this rule: false landmarks never sit orthogonally adjacent to one another. (This is not guaranteed by the deal, so it only works if you enforce it during setup: after dealing the 36 cards, secretly move any adjacent ♠ 2–7 cards apart — it is fine to look only at the spades to do this.)

With this rule, every false landmark you find tells you something: its four orthogonal neighbors are known safe. Mark them. The map becomes a logic puzzle as well as a race.

The pressure

The shortest path from corner to corner is 10 moves. You have 20 Provisions. That is 10 spare — enough for three false landmarks or ten probes, but not both. The game is the negotiation between knowing and going.

06The Debt

Push-your-luck

Time: 30–45 minTrack: Gold · Health · DayDifficulty: Medium

You owe a man named Corvo eighty gold, and Corvo does not extend credit. You have ten days. Every day you can work honestly, gamble stupidly, hustle dangerously, or hide. Corvo's collectors are always somewhere in the city.

Setup

Shuffle the deck. On your ledger write:

  • Gold: 0
  • Health: 10
  • Day: 1
  • Debt: 80 Gold, due at the end of Day 10.

Each Day

  1. Choose one action (see table).
  2. The Collector. Unless you Hid, draw 1 card. If it is ♠ or ♣, a collector finds you: lose Health equal to ceil(rank ÷ 3). Rank values: Ace = 11, face cards = 10.
  3. Advance the day.

If the deck runs out, shuffle the discard pile into a new deck.

ActionEffectRisk
Work Gain 7 Gold. None beyond the normal collector draw.
Gamble Draw 3 cards. If the sum of ♥♦ ranks exceeds the sum of ♠♣ ranks, gain 20 Gold. If black is higher, lose 12 Gold (minimum 0). On a tie, gain 5 Gold. Normal collector draw.
Hustle Draw 2 cards. Gain Gold equal to their combined rank values. If either card is ♠ or ♣, a collector notices — draw a second collector card this evening. High risk of violence.
Hide Gain nothing. Skip the collector draw entirely. None — but the clock still moves.

A Friend in Low Places. Once per game, ignore one collector hit entirely.

The End

VictoryAt the end of Day 10, you hold at least 80 Gold. You pay Corvo and walk away.
DefeatHealth reaches 0 — the collectors find you first. Or Day 10 ends with less than 80 Gold.
Skip TownAt any time, you may abandon the debt and flee. The game ends immediately. Score = your current Gold. You are a fugitive, and you did not win — but you survived.
Score (win)Gold remaining after paying the debt, plus Health remaining × 2.
The pressure

Working every day for ten days yields 70 gold — not nearly enough. You must gamble or hustle, and both cost you Health. The real question each morning is not "how do I make money" but "how much of myself am I willing to spend to make it faster."

07The Gravekeeper

Quiet horror

Time: 20–30 minTrack: Rest · Dread · DayDifficulty: Medium

You are the keeper of a small churchyard. The dead arrive, one by one, and each must be buried where they belong. Bury them right and they sleep. Bury them wrong and they linger — and a yard full of the restless does not stay quiet for long.

Setup

Shuffle the deck. On your ledger write:

  • Rest: 6
  • Dread: 0
  • Day: 1

Draw a 3×3 grid of empty plots on your paper. That is the churchyard.

Set the patron suits. Draw 3 cards, one per row from top to bottom — the suit of each card is that row's patron suit, fixed for the whole game. Write each suit at its row's edge, then shuffle the three cards back into the deck.

Each Day

Play 9 days, one card per day, until all nine plots are filled.

  1. One of the dead arrives. Draw 1 card.
  2. Bury them. Place the card in any empty plot and write its rank and suit in the plot.
  3. Judge the burial. A card placed in a row whose patron suit matches its own suit is at rest. A card placed in a row of a different suit is restless: +1 Dread immediately.
  4. Face cards. A face card (J, Q, K) placed in a matching row gives +1 Rest — the village is grateful. A face card in a mismatched row gives +2 Dread instead of +1.
  5. Advance the day.

The Rites

Once per day, after placing, you may use one rite:

RiteEffect
The StoneMove one placed card to a different empty plot. Judge it again against its new row: if it now rests, remove 1 Dread gained from that card; if it is now restless, gain Dread as normal (2 for a face card).
The SongLook at the next card in the deck before tomorrow. You may not change it, only see it.
The CoinSpend 1 Rest (you must have at least 1): remove today's card from its plot, discard it, and draw a replacement to place in the same plot instead. Any Dread gained from the removed card is forgiven; the replacement is judged fresh.

Winning & Losing

After Day 9All nine plots are filled. Read the Dread:
Dread 0–2“The yard sleeps soundly.” You win.
Dread 3–5“They stir, but they stay down.” You win, uneasily.
Dread 6 or moreThe dead rise. You lose.
ScoreRest − Dread.
The pressure

Nine cards, four suits, three rows — one suit will always be wrong somewhere. The game is deciding which row to sacrifice before the deck decides for you.

Tuning & Variants

Every game here is deliberately tuned to be winnable but not easy on a first play. If a game feels too harsh or too soft, adjust one number — never more than one at a time.

Lantern KeepersStart Oil 12; Wick 3Start Oil 18; sheltering a red visitor gives +3 Nerve

Campaign Mode

For a longer session, link three games of the same type into a campaign:

  • Lantern ×3: each survival grants +2 starting Oil and +1 starting Wick for the next.
  • Auction ×3: carry your Coins forward; rivals reset their purses each night.
  • Machine ×3: your score total is your campaign score; a Masterwork grants an extra turn next machine.
  • Regent ×3: each successful year grants +2 starting Treasury for the next.
  • Cartographer ×3: each treasure recovered adds +3 Provisions to the next expedition.
  • Debt ×3: each paid debt reduces the next by 20 Gold, but Corvo remembers — collectors deal +1 damage next time.
  • Gravekeeper ×3: each quiet yard grants +1 starting Rest for the next.
