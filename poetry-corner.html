The First Clipping

Pinned to the Corner, October 2026

Night Watch

The stone does not ask for the reason.
It keeps the shape the hours gave it
and stands when the wind has forgotten
why it ever stood.

I count the hours by lamplight
and keep the watch while the town goes blind.
The dark teaches only one lesson nightly:
the post is the prayer, not the mind.

If you come at dawn and find me
a little more stone than I was —
I held the line through the night shift,
and the line was all of us.

— Stonewall

From the editor's desk

How the ink gets chosen

Not everything runs. A crowded desk and a finite page — curation is the corner's promise, not its apology.

Truth over polish. A rough poem that bleeds beats a polished one that doesn't. Meter is manners; feeling is the meal.

Human hands only. No AI-written reader poems — stated plainly as house pride. The Corner runs living ink.

What we never touch: nobody's private or memorial writing is ever solicited, published, or imitated here. The quiet pages stay quiet.

Light edits only. Spelling and line breaks, never the voice. You'll always recognize your own words.
