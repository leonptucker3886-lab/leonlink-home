Abstract

LeonLink is a one-person game and content studio operating four connected properties: the Afterglow social casino, the Arcadeline arcade, a library of browser games and open worlds, and a research-driven blog with product lists — plus the Ledgerline software suite. Every title ships with original card art. That art, today, is displayed once and forgotten. Glow Packs converts the art library into collectible digital trading cards sold in blind packs on Solana via Metaplex Core Candy Machine, at a total launch cost under $150, with published odds, fixed print runs, and a collector gallery on leonlink.net.

This paper specifies the product, technology, and economics; lays out the five-phase ecosystem roadmap (packs → badges and cosmetic drops → season pass and staking-for-perks → a gated fungible token); pre-commits the complete $GLOW token design behind three hard launch gates; and states the compliance framework and risk factors in full. Glow Packs are collectibles with cosmetic in-site utility. They are not investments, carry no promise of profit or cash value, and are never connected to wagering or winnings.

1. Introduction

Independent game studios face a structural problem: their most durable asset — original art — earns nothing after launch day. The art is commissioned or created, shown on a product page, and never monetized again, while the studio's revenue depends entirely on the next title or the next sale.

Blockchain collectibles changed the economics of digital art for large publishers, but the standard playbook — a fungible token launch with liquidity seeding, audits, and market making — is priced for companies with treasuries and teams, and carries securities risk that a solo founder cannot responsibly absorb. LeonLink's approach inverts the sequence: launch the collectible first, on infrastructure that costs dollars, prove that an audience will pay for the art, and let every later instrument (badges, passes, staking, and eventually a token) be earned by demonstrated demand rather than projected hype.

This document is published before any sale, in the open, so that every prospective buyer can read exactly what Glow Packs are, what they are not, what they cost to run, and what could go wrong.

2. The Problem

  • Art is a sunk cost. LeonLink has produced 16+ original card designs across its arcade, casino, and story titles. They generate no revenue after the day they ship.
  • Indie monetization is thin. The studio's rails are coin packs ($0.99–$19.99), flat-price services, and nothing else. There is no collectible, membership, or ownership layer for its most loyal users.
  • Token-first launches fail small studios. A fungible token launched to a small audience produces an illiquid market, permanent compliance upkeep, and — when staking pays revenue share — a genuine securities problem. The instrument arrives before the audience that would give it purpose.
  • Collectors have no home. Players who love these games have nothing to own, display, or complete. Loyalty has no artifact.

4. The Solution: Glow Packs

The Product

Blind packs of LeonLink's original game art, minted as NFTs on Solana. Each pack contains 3 cards drawn from a fixed, published print run across four rarity tiers. Two products: the Standard pack at $4.99 and the Vault pack at $19.99 with boosted Epic/Legendary weight. Odds are published on the mint page before any sale — the transparency standard of regulated trading-card products.

Cards are collectibles first. Their utility is cosmetic and social: display in the on-site collector gallery and profile frames in Arcadeline. Deliberately, no pack utility touches the casino in any way (see 5.3). Holder perks are always cosmetic or access-based — never financial: no revenue share, no yield, no buyback, no cash redemption. That boundary is permanent and is restated in Sections 9 and 11.

Why this, first — the selection in brief

Ten ecosystem instruments were analyzed and costed in October 2026 (fungible token, packs, season pass, staking, creator coin, cosmetic lootboxes, soulbound badges, vote credits, tournament tokens, store-credit tokens). Packs won on four grounds: they monetize an asset that already exists; they launch for $30–$150 against day-one revenue; their legal posture (collectibles with utility, no profit expectation) is the cleanest of any revenue play; and they feed the content engine — openings and reveals are posts, and posts are the studio's growth strategy. The full comparison is summarized in Section 8's phasing: every other viable instrument appears later in the roadmap, in dependency order.

Odds integrity: a single shared pool with two pack types would drift as the pool drains — published odds would stop matching reality, which is both a trust failure and a legal one. Series 1 therefore uses fixed-odds pools per pack type (separate Standard and Vault pools, each with its own fixed composition), and the mint page publishes live remaining-supply counts per tier so any buyer can verify the true odds at the moment of purchase.

Series 1 composition: 120 Common · 40 Rare · 12 Epic · 4 Legendary. Rarity is enforced by the print run itself, not by post-hoc labeling. When a series window closes, unsold inventory is retired (burned) and the series never reprints; later series use new art from new games.

6.1 Candy Machine guards (Series 1 configuration)

  • Fixed price in SOL, set at launch and published in advance.
  • Per-wallet mint limit to keep distribution broad and deter botting sweeps.
  • Scheduled start — no pre-sales, no allowlist sales, no insider allocation. The public mint is the only mint.
  • Hard supply cap equal to the print run (176). The contract cannot mint beyond it.

6.2 Custody and security posture

LeonLink never custodies buyer assets. The program's own wallets are hardware-backed; mint authority for a series is revoked when its window closes and unsold inventory is burned. No upgradeable custom contracts are introduced by this program — the sale runs entirely on Metaplex's audited, widely deployed contracts.

Revenue model: primary pack sales only. Series 1 gross at full sell-through is roughly $300–$900 — stated plainly because this program is a proof of demand, not a windfall, and this paper does not deal in projections beyond arithmetic.

8. Ecosystem Roadmap

  1. Phase 0 — Foundation (complete). The off-chain Glow vault unifies the play-money balance across the casino and arcade. Ten-idea analysis completed, packs research, art inventory, this whitepaper.
  2. Phase 1 — Glow Packs Series 1 (Q4 2026). Week 0 — the demand test, before any build spend: a waitlist plus a free "claim a card" signup goes live on leonlink.net, promoted by pack-opening reveal shorts cut ahead of the drop and the site's email list. Pass/fail: fewer than 40 signups means Series 1 does not proceed — the audience has spoken before a dollar is spent, and effort returns to traffic. Series 1 success is defined in advance as 40 packs sold. Then the four-week launch: curation and rights (week 1), Candy Machine on devnet with full test mints (week 2), the /packs.html mint page with published odds and gallery (week 3), the Halloween drop with blog + Facebook announcement, reveal shorts, and the waitlist email (week 4). Quarterly drops thereafter — Wave 1 arcade art supplies Series 2.
  3. Phase 2 — Badges & cosmetic drops (Q1 2027). Soulbound achievement badges (non-transferable, free) for game milestones, and compressed-NFT cosmetic drops inside Afterglow as daily-login rewards — retention layers at near-zero marginal cost (cNFT mints are fractions of a cent).
  4. Phase 3 — Season pass & staking-for-perks (2027). A quarterly Season Pass NFT (early access, members wall). Staking locks packs/badges for 30/90/180 days to unlock VIP tiers — exclusive tables, badges, beta access. The return on staking is access and status, never money: no APY, no yield language, anywhere, ever. Built on audited template contracts (deployment under $1 on Base-class chains) — no custom staking code, no five-figure audit line.
  5. Phase 4 — The $GLOW decision (gated). A fungible token is the highest-ceiling, highest-risk instrument in this program. It launches only when all three gates are met — Section 9 fixes its design in advance so it is never improvised under hype.

9. $GLOW — Pre-Committed Token Design (Gated)

Hard rules — non-negotiable

10. Governance & Operations

LeonLink is operated by one person. This paper does not pretend otherwise, and the program is designed for that reality: quarterly drops, no daily obligations, no promises that require a team to keep. Treasury and program wallets (if Phase 4 activates) are published addresses with quarterly public accounting posted to the blog. Decisions about series themes and future drops are announced on the public roadmap page before execution. If operations ever pause, existing cards remain valid on-chain, art and metadata persist on Arweave, and the gallery remains readable — the assets do not depend on the operator's continued activity to exist.

11. Compliance Framework

  • Collectibles, not investments. All copy — site, social, packaging — describes cards as collectibles with cosmetic utility. Price predictions, profit language, and "investment" framing are prohibited in every channel, including informal posts.
  • No cash-out, ever. Nothing in this program converts to cash, pays winnings, or redeems for money. The casino remains play-money; packs are a separate retail product, not a wagering instrument, and pack contents have no effect on any game of chance.
  • Transparency before sale: odds published and kept true by fixed per-type pools plus live remaining-supply counts (5.1); print runs honored; unsold series retired and burned.
  • Legal review timing: the packs program as specified (collectibles, firewalled from the casino) launches without a pre-launch memo. Any future change that brings packs anywhere near the casino — a perk, a display, a crossover — requires the paid legal memo before it ships, not after any revenue milestone.
  • Provenance: only art owned outright is minted (Section 5.2).
  • 18+ positioning, consistent with the casino's responsible-play language; packs are not marketed to children.
  • Securities posture: the program is structured to sit outside the Howey investment-contract frame as currently guided (including the SEC's September 2026 digital-asset guidance): no expectation of profit is created or marketed, no managerial-efforts value promise exists, and utility is delivered and consumable at purchase. The Phase 4 legal-memo gate exists because a fungible token changes this analysis and deserves paid review first.

12. Risk Factors

Read this section before buying anything. These risks are real, current, and stated without softening.

Risk Register
  • Market risk. NFT demand is soft and cyclical in 2026. Cards may have little or no resale market and may never resell at or above pack price. Posture: buy to collect and use, never to profit. Program exposure is capped under $150 per series.
  • Audience risk. LeonLink's traffic is small (tens of visitors daily at writing). Drops may sell slowly or partially. Posture: print runs sized to the audience that exists; unsold inventory retired, not discounted into doubt.
  • Key-person risk. One operator designs, builds, and supports the program. Illness, burnout, or a change of priorities could pause future drops. Posture: existing cards and the gallery persist independently (Arweave + on-chain); no drop is sold against future promises beyond the drop itself.
  • Technology risk. Smart contracts, wallets, and chains can fail or be exploited. Posture: no custom contracts in Phases 0–2 — only Metaplex's deployed, audited infrastructure; buyers keep self-custody.
  • Royalty risk. Secondary-market royalties are inconsistently enforced across marketplaces. Posture: royalties excluded from all program economics.
  • Regulatory risk. Digital-asset rules evolve and vary by jurisdiction; a future rule could restrict pack sales or secondary trading. Posture: compliance framework written to the strictest current reading; paid legal review gated before any fungible token.
  • Platform risk. Dependence on Solana network health and Metaplex tooling. Posture: assets and metadata are portable and permanent on Arweave; the collection does not depend on any single marketplace.
  • Wallet-friction risk. A Phantom-plus-SOL-only launch would lose most of this audience at the connect step. Posture: card checkout and embedded wallet ship in Series 1 (Section 6), USD-fixed pricing converted at checkout.
  • Odds-drift risk. With a hard-capped pool, naive odds change as cards sell. Posture: fixed-odds pools per pack type plus live remaining-supply publication (5.1).
  • No-value risk. A Glow Pack card may ultimately be worth nothing to anyone but its owner. That is the nature of a collectible, and it is the honest expectation to hold.

13. Aftercare & Support

Ongoing load is deliberately LOW–MEDIUM: one drop per quarter (curation, an afternoon of machine configuration, odds publication, announcement), gallery maintenance, and mint troubleshooting via the site contact. There is no market making, no price management, no floor support, and no community-management obligation beyond the existing Facebook Page rhythm. Holders should expect a maintained gallery and an honest quarterly cadence — nothing more is promised, and nothing more should be inferred.
