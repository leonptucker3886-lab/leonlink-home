Lume has two markets

Users pay for legibility. Collectors pay for originality. Confusing the two is how a relume turns a valuable vintage dial into a discounted one.

Pre-1960s

Radium paint

Radioactive zinc-sulfide that ages cream, beige or honey. The value is in an untouched original dial and hands—not brightness.

COLLECTOR PLAY · ORIGINAL PATINA
1960s–~1998

Tritium paint

12.3-year half-life; glow fades as amber patina develops. Even “T Swiss T” or issued circled-T examples can command premiums. Reluming can mean a 25%+ haircut.

COLLECTOR PLAY · NEVER RELUME
Modern constant glow

Tritium tubes

Sealed glass tubes glow without charging for roughly 20 years. Marathon, Ball and Luminox use them. Superb utility; not the same collector premium as old paint.

USER PLAY · NIGHT LEGIBILITY
Modern rechargeable

LumiNova / LumiBrite

Non-radioactive pigments charge in light, shine brightest initially and remain color-stable. BGW9, C3 and Seiko LumiBrite are the practical standard.

USER PLAY · BRIGHTEST BURST

How to buy without donating margin

The watch is only half the trade. Box, papers, bracelet links, originality and patience determine whether your “deal” survives resale.

01

Buy the second owner

For high-production Swiss and Japanese pieces, wait for the first 25–45% decline. Oris Aquis, Ball Marvelight and Hamilton Intra-Matic are prime examples.

02

Pay for the set

Full set improves liquidity. Grand Seiko 9F and limited microbrands show especially wide spreads when box, papers or limited-edition proof is missing.

03

Scarcity must be real

Batch drops, allocation and documented issue history can support prices. A “limited” engraving without a waiting market cannot.

04

Never average asking prices

Chrono24 and dealer asks can sit above clearing prices. Negotiate from completed sales and recent forum comps when possible.

05

Originality beats brightness

On vintage military and painted-lume watches, do not polish or relume. Honest patina and issued provenance are the asset.

06

Keep a service reserve

A low entry can be erased by overdue service, damaged crystals or missing links. Price those before judging retention.

Read the numbers correctly

This is a dated market snapshot, not an appraisal and not investment advice. Watch markets are thin, condition-sensitive and unusually vulnerable to optimistic asks.

What was compared

Brand MSRP was compared with 2025–2026 web-indexed dealer asks, Chrono24 listings, eBay listings/sold evidence where available, WatchUSeek, WatchRecon, MyWatchMart and regional marketplace comps. Retention is a band because condition, full-set status, dial, bracelet and geography materially change price.

  • Gray-market prices move quickly. Recheck current comps before buying.
