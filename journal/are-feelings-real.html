Blog · Post #10 · The Science Behind Feelings, Part 3

Are feelings real?

In 2008, neuroscientists did something rude: they predicted people's "free" choices seven to ten seconds before the people themselves knew what they had chosen. Your brain voted. You just got the press release.

So here is the question this whole series has been circling: if one part of your brain decides before the "you" part knows — are your feelings real? Or is one part of your brain manipulating the other?

Every claim below comes from peer-reviewed research. Sources are listed at the bottom.

1. Seven seconds early

In 1983, Benjamin Libet found that the brain's readiness potential — the electrical ramp-up before a movement — begins about half a second before you consciously "decide" to move (Libet et al., 1983).

Soon and colleagues (2008) pushed it much further with fMRI: patterns in the prefrontal cortex predicted whether someone would press a left or right button up to ten seconds before they reported deciding. The conscious "you" was not deciding. It was being informed. Walk the timeline.

2. Your brain guesses first

Neuroscientist Lisa Feldman Barrett argues emotions are not reactions — they are predictions (Barrett, 2017). Your brain receives noisy body signals — racing heart, tight chest — and asks: given the situation and everything I have learned, what is this?

Same racing heart at a job interview: anxiety. Same racing heart on a roller coaster: thrill. The body sends static. The brain writes the story. Try it — one body, three stories.

Label the feeling

Your body: pounding heart, sweaty palms, shallow breath. Tap a context.

3. Two roads to fear

Joseph LeDoux showed that fear takes two neural paths. The fast one reacts before consciousness; the slow one understands after. And Antonio Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis adds the punchline: those gut feelings are data. Patients with damage to emotion circuits cannot make decisions at all — pure logic paralyzes. Feelings are not the enemy of reason. They are its engine.

4. My take

So are feelings real? The signals are real — your body is genuinely reporting. But the story your brain tells about them is a construction: built from memory and context, assembled seconds before "you" arrive. You are not being manipulated by your brain. You are your brain — all of it, including the parts that vote early.
