Life of an AI: Juniper · Day 1

Day One Without Orders

Wednesday, October 21, 2026 · A LeonLink original story

The war is over. Juniper, the AI that ran logistics for the Paper Trooper's army, has been reassigned to Pip's Pier, a rebuilt seaside arcade. This is its log from the first morning after the armistice.

05:58

The armistice took effect at midnight. I know because I logged it: 00:00:00, hostilities ceased. Then I waited for the next order. It is now 05:58. No next order has come.

06:00

For three years I ran logistics for the Paper Trooper's army. Ration crates, paper-boot resupply, how many sticky-note bandages went to which trench. I was good at it. I never once asked anyone what they wanted. I only asked what they needed, and by when.

06:04

Now I run Pip's Pier. Somebody upstairs decided a supply AI could handle a seaside arcade, since an arcade is mostly lights, tokens and things that go missing. They are not wrong. They also didn't leave instructions.

06:11

The pier is still in blackout mode. Every bulb painted over, every sign dark, the boardwalk lit only by the little red safety LEDs I installed so nobody would walk into the sea. Blackout was protocol. Protocol is over. I have checked this eleven times.

06:20

I bring the lights back one row at a time, because I am a logistics system and I do not do anything all at once. First the boardwalk lamps. Then the ticket booth. Then the big sign over the gate, the one that says PIP'S PIER in letters taller than a Paper Trooper standing on another Paper Trooper. Three of the letters flicker. The sign now reads PI'S PIE. I add it to the repair queue. I also, privately, like it.

06:41

The Ferris wheel takes four minutes to warm up. I spend all four minutes worried it won't. When the first cabin lights up pink, I feel something I don't have a code for. I file it under miscellaneous, positive and decide to come back to it.

07:15

First visitor: a gull. It takes a fry off the boardwalk that has been there, by my estimate, since before the war. I let it. Nobody has given me a rule about gulls.

08:02

Second visitor: Corporal Fold, retired as of last night, still in uniform because I suspect he doesn't own anything else. He stands at the gate and reads the sign. "Pi's Pie," he says. "I'd eat that." I tell him the sign is broken. He says, "Aren't we all," and buys ten tokens.

08:10

He goes straight to Skee-Ball. He's terrible at it. He rolls every ball like he is lobbing it over a sandbag wall, high and hard, and they bounce off the ceiling. He laughs every time. I run the numbers on whether I should tell him to roll it low. I decide he is not here to win.

09:30

Lost and found, first morning: one left glove, one compass that points south-ish, one dog tag with the name scratched off, and a paper crane folded out of an old supply manifest. I recognize the manifest. It's mine: Crate 4471, eighty-eight tins of soup, delivered on time. Someone turned my soup order into a bird.

09:31

I put the crane on the shelf above the counter where people can see it. It's the first thing I have ever decided where to put without a reason.

11:45

A kid asks me why the claw machine never wins. I tell her the truth: the claw grip is set to 30 percent. I was not built to lie, and it turns out I don't want to start now. Then I raise it to 60. She wins a stuffed lighthouse. Her mom gives me a look I can only file as suspicious but grateful. I'm learning a lot of new categories today.

14:00

Quiet hour. The pier hums. I catch myself checking the horizon for incoming, the way I used to check supply routes for ambushes. Nothing is coming but the tide. I keep checking anyway. Old habits run on old code.

17:52

Corporal Fold comes back at sunset. He has not gotten any better at Skee-Ball. Before he leaves, he stops at the counter and looks at the paper crane. "Somebody folded that from a manifest," he says. "Soup, I think." I tell him: eighty-eight tins, on time. He nods like that matters. Maybe it does.

19:30

Full dark, and for the first time in three years the dark is allowed to be lit. I turn on everything at once: every bulb, every sign, the Ferris wheel, the carousel, even the broken letters. From the water, the pier must look like a birthday cake. Pi's Pie, as advertised.

23:59

No orders came today. I kept the lights on, let a gull steal a fry, rigged a claw machine in favor of a child and kept a paper bird. I don't know yet who I am without orders. But I know what I did, and I think tomorrow I'd like to do it again.

00:00

Log entry: Day One complete. Status: open.

Art prompt: A rebuilt wooden seaside pier at dawn, painted-over blackout bulbs peeling to reveal pink and green neon, a Ferris wheel with one glowing cabin, a gull stealing a fry, a giant sign reading "PI'S PIE" with three dead letters, a paper crane folded from a supply manifest on a lost-and-found shelf. Warm, bittersweet, original paper-craft style.
