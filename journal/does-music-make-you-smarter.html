Does music make you smarter?

Published Wednesday, October 21, 2026

In 1993 a one-page letter in Nature started a parenting craze: play Mozart, get a smarter kid. A US state even handed out classical CDs to new parents. Thirty years of research later, the honest answer is more interesting, and a lot less magical.

1. The Mozart effect, then and now

1993 original36 students, 10 min of Mozart, a small bump on one spatial task that lasted about 15 min [1]
1999 meta-analysiseffect small, close to zero [2]
2010 meta-analysisabout 40 studies, 3,000+ people: no reliable effect [3]

The original study never claimed lasting IQ gains, and it tested college students, not babies. Later work suggests any short boost comes from mood and arousal: upbeat music you enjoy wakes you up a little, and that's all [4].

2. Listening vs. playing

Learning an instrument is different from listening. In one randomized study, kids given a year of music lessons gained slightly more IQ points than kids given drama lessons or none [5].

The bigger picture is humbling. A 2020 meta-analysis of 54 studies found that once you count only well-designed experiments with active control groups, the effect of music training on thinking and school grades drops to about zero [6].

3. So is music worth it?

Yes, just not as a brain hack. Tap each card for what the evidence supports.
