Are we simulated?

RED PILL AVAILABLE

In 2016, Elon Musk stood on a stage and said the odds that we're living in "base reality" — the real, physical, un-simulated world — are one in billions. Not a joke, not a tweet. His actual stated position, on the record, twice.

He's not alone. An Oxford philosopher proved the logic in 2003. Neil deGrasse Tyson has given it 50/50. A Nobel physicist, a famous sci-fi author, and the man who wrote the book on consciousness all say some version of: you can't rule it out.

So let's do what this blog does. The argument, the quotes, the evidence for and against — then you run your own numbers and vote.

Quotes below are from public interviews, debates and papers, and sources are listed at the bottom.

4. Run your own odds

Bostrom's math, simplified. If civilizations can build ancestor simulations, and some do, and they run a lot of them — then most minds like yours are simulated ones. Move the sliders, or try a preset.

How the math works: take the chance a civilization reaches simulation tech, times the chance such civilizations actually run ancestor simulations, times how many each one runs. Divide that by itself-plus-one (the one base reality). That's your odds of being simulated.

5. My take

Here's where I've landed. Musk's Pong argument is the one I can't shake: forty years from two rectangles and a dot to photorealistic worlds with millions of players. Whatever the rate of progress from here, "indistinguishable from reality" is downstream of it — unless we wipe ourselves out first, which is Bostrom's whole point.

But Chalmers has the other half of it: you can't prove we're not in one, because any evidence could be simulated. An unfalsifiable idea is a fun idea, not a proven one. I put it where Tyson ended up: somewhere around a coin flip, and the coin is still in the air.

And honestly? If we are in a simulation, the programmer has a weird sense of humor — and I still have to pay rent. Base reality or not, the move is the same: build things.
