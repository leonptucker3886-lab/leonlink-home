What is AI?

Everyone talks about AI. Almost no one can say what it is. Strip away the hype and the definition is almost boring: AI is prediction at scale. Given some input, predict the most likely output — the next word, the next pixel, the next move. Everything else is engineering around that trick.

Once you see it, you cannot unsee it. And the trick is older than you think.

Every claim below comes from peer-reviewed research. Sources are listed at the bottom.

1. Prediction machines

A neural network is layers of tiny adjustable knobs. You show it millions of examples, it twists the knobs until its predictions stop being wrong. That is the whole miracle: adjust knobs, reduce error, repeat a billion times. Tap the layers.

2. The transformer turn

In 2017, a Google paper titled "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017) introduced the transformer — the architecture behind every chatbot you have used. Its trick: instead of reading words in order, it weighs every word against every other word at once. Attention.

The result is autocomplete with a memory. Try it — finish the sentence the way a transformer would: by probability, not by thought.

3. A 70-year overnight success

AI did not arrive in 2022. It arrived in slow waves, each one declared a revolution, most ending in a funding winter. Walk the timeline.

4. My take

AI is not a mind. It is the best prediction engine ever built — and prediction turns out to cover a shocking amount of what we called thinking. The question was never really "what is it." It is "what do we do with it." That is part two.
