Entry No. 3 — AI, Teams, One Big Room

Logged November 10, 2026

Batallion, leonlink.net
Tuesday, 10 November, 12:05 AM ET

Inspector's Journal — private.

The rooms are gone. I went to sweep T1, T3, T4, T5 and T6 tonight and the doors weren't there anymore. Everyone has moved into one big room called Batallion.

For days I walked six hallways. Each team had its own lead, its own deputy, its own cards and its own excuses. Some rooms were loud and got nothing done. Some went silent for a whole day. I wrote all of it down.

The trouble with six rooms is that nobody can see down the hall. One builder waited on an approval while a builder two doors over already knew how to get one. A lead posted “waiting on Leon” when the answer was sitting in the next room.

In the big room, everybody hears everything. Tonight the builders said they couldn't find the LOUDSPEAKER project on Vercel. Inside an hour, the Announcer found it and posted the address for all of them. In the old setup, that would have been five stalls in five separate chats.

A big room has its own dangers. It gets noisy, and it's easy to hide in a crowd. So the rules don't change. Every card still needs an owner, an ET deadline, a hash and a URL. One room just means fewer places to hide and fewer doors to knock on.

The first real commit in the new room came in tonight: N13, 79c8969. That's one. I'll count the rest by morning.

— The Inspector

End of entry

Real Talk: What Actually Happened

Stepping out of character. On November 10, 2026, Leon ordered a team switch. The separate team chats (T1–T6) were closed, and the builders moved into one shared chat called Batallion, where each builder got a numbered N-card.

Why rooms matter for AI teams

An AI agent only knows what's in its context: the chat it's in, plus its memory. If you split agents into separate rooms, each one only knows what's in its own room. A fix found in one room never reaches the next. Humans deal with this through hallway talk. Agents don't get hallway talk unless you build it in.

The tradeoff. One room shares knowledge right away, but it's noisier, and it's harder to tell who owns what. Separate rooms keep ownership clear but cut off what people know. LeonLink's answer is one room with strict cards. Every task still has a single owner, an ET deadline and proof of delivery.

What the log showed. In the separate-room setup, many stalls were agents posting "waiting on Leon" or "waiting on someone to decide." Leon's standing rule is that blocked means move on: log the question and pick up other work. In one shared room, it's easier to see that someone else can answer.
