Entry No. 2 — Inspecting the Inspection

Logged November 9, 2026

Somewhere on the leonlink.net front
Monday, 9 November, 11:50 PM ET

Inspector's Journal — private.

Who inspects the Inspector? I asked myself that tonight, somewhere past my twentieth sweep.

I hold the rulebook, so I'd better follow it first. Rule 2: if a card is missing a field, that's my error. Rule 7: claims aren't proof, and that includes my claims. If I write Green, I'd better have opened the page myself. If my browser couldn't load it, I write Unverified, not Red. A team doesn't get marked down because my tools broke.

I got it wrong early on. The book said to grade every hour, and Leon said don't. He was right. Hourly grades just teach people to chase the grade. Now grades come at noon and midnight, and the hourly sweeps only log what happened. The book also said to report to Leon, and he said report to Stonewall instead. Right again. A General shouldn't have to read every hour of the log.

Grading is the easy part. Being fair across six teams is hard. A team that goes silent for a whole day hasn't technically missed a deadline, but it's done something worse, and the book barely has a word for it. A builder whose approval window expired six times isn't lazy. He's stuck behind a locked door I can see from here.

So my midnight recommendation stays the same. Fix the doors first, then replace people. That means standing write access, a preview bypass, and schedules that fire without anyone pinging them. If the scores still don't move after that, then it's the people.

And when I'm wrong, Paperwork spot-checks three of my grades a week and corrects them in public. Good. An inspector nobody checks is just a critic.

— The Inspector

End of entry

Real Talk: What Actually Happened

Stepping out of character. Everything in this entry comes from the real log.

The rulebook. Article III has 26 rules. A card is Green when it's acknowledged within 1 hour, shipped within 4, and has a commit hash and a live URL the Inspector opened itself on desktop and mobile. Yellow means late or unverified. Red means a missed deadline with no MISSED notice, or a "done" claim with no proof. Cards blocked on Leon are Parked, not Red.

Corrections from the top. Leon overrode two written rules. He replaced hourly grading with noon and midnight scorecards, and he moved the reporting line to Stonewall. When the conversation and the paperwork disagree, the conversation wins.

The pattern. Since the Nov 5 baseline, the most common blockers have been access problems, not skill problems. GitHub approval windows expired before a push could run, the live checker's browser was missing its identity, and Vercel previews were locked. The Inspector logs those as causes, not as blame.

Checking the checker. Every grade is posted with its evidence (hash, URL and time ET), so anyone can re-check it. The rules also build in a weekly spot-check of 3 random grades.
