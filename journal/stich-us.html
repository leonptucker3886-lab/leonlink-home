Stich-Us

A GUIDED PATH BACK TO TRUST

Stich-Us is nine acts for two people after one of them broke trust. One side is the person who was hurt. The other is the person who broke it. Every act has work for both of them, and neither side gets to skip their part.

There is an app that walks a couple through it together — private rooms, a question queue, and each act sealed in turn before the next one opens. If you want the guided version, it is here:

Open the Stich-Us app →

But you do not need a computer to do this. Everything in the app is printed here, act by act, in order — the whole path, free to read. Read it on your phone. Write the answers on paper. Do the work together. That is what this series is for. If it finds and helps one couple, it has done its job.

Stich-Us grew out of an earlier post here — Partner Cheating?, on why people cheat, the research and the red flags. That post asked why it happens. This series is the path back after it does.

Stich-Us was built by Leon Tucker, and is presented by LeonLink.

How to use this series

Read the acts in order, one at a time, and do not skip ahead. Each act builds on the one before it.

Each act has tasks for the person who broke trust, and a section for the person who was hurt. The tasks are written to be done in writing — the point is saying the thing out loud, in the first person, without handing any of it back.

The questions in Act III are the heart of it. The appendix below has twelve questions to start with, and four rules for what makes an answer count.

The nine acts

Act I · Part 1 of 9
The Weight

Take full responsibility. — No conditions. No receipts. No “but”.

Act II · Part 2 of 9
The Record

Admit what they have done. — A record with holes in it is a second lie with better manners.

Read Act II →

Act III · Part 3 of 9
Every Question, Every Time

Answer all questions, every time. — The question is not the wound. The refusal to answer is.

Read Act III →

Act IV · Part 4 of 9
The Why Underneath

Understand why you did it. — A reason explains. An excuse excuses. Learn the difference or you'll keep making excuses.

Read Act IV →

Act V · Part 5 of 9
No More Excuses

Stop making excuses and casting blame. Don't blame anyone. Don't rationalise. Bear the weight. — You do not get to grade their reaction.

Read Act V →

Act VI · Part 6 of 9
The Inventory

Search inside yourself for a complete list of what you regret, and identify who you hurt. — If the list only contains things that would upset them, it's a performance.

Read Act VI →

Act VII · Part 7 of 9
The Apology

Apologise sincerely. Stop hurting people. Accept judgment. Changed behaviour. — “I'm sorry you feel that way” is not an apology. It's a bill.

Read Act VII →

Act VIII · Part 8 of 9
New Walls

Accept limits. Live with the insecurity you created. — Boundaries are not punishment. They're scaffolding.

Read Act VIII →

Act IX · Part 9 of 9
Grace Under the Storm

Take your partner's moods and suspicions with grace. You broke the trust; this weather is yours. — You caused the weather. You can stand in it.

Appendix — the questions to start with

Twelve questions from the app’s question queue. The hurt side asks; the rebuilding side answers each one in full, one at a time.

The personWho was it, and how did you meet?
The timelineHow long did it go on, and when exactly did it end?
The placesWhere did it happen?
SafetyDid you use protection?
The liesWhat did you tell them about me?
The intentDid you ever think about leaving me for them?
The endingWhose idea was it to stop?
The comparisonWas there anything you did with them that you've refused me?
The lyingWhich nights did you lie to my face about it?
The circleWho else knew?
The presentDo you still think about them?
The feelingWhat did you feel the first time — and the last?

What makes a good answer

SpecificNames, dates, places — or an honest “I don't know” plus what you'll check.
No blameNothing in the answer points at them, their body, their mood, or their schedule.
No “but”If you need to add a caveat, it goes in the next sentence, or nowhere.
Their questionAnswer what was asked. Not the adjacent thing that's easier to say.
