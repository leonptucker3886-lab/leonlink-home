Life without money

Follow the automation curve far enough and it hits a wall called money. If machines do the work, wages stop being how people eat. Economists have argued about this for a century — and in the last decade, governments finally started running the experiment.

This is not science fiction. It is pilot data.

Every claim below comes from peer-reviewed research. Sources are listed at the bottom.

1. When work ends

Frey and Osborne (2017) estimated 47% of US jobs face a high risk of computerization. Previous waves automated muscle, then routine mind-work. This wave automates prediction itself — which was the last safe harbor.

Here is the uncomfortable math: an economy needs consumers, and consumers need income, and income has meant jobs. Break the chain anywhere and something has to replace wages — or the economy eats itself. The leading candidate is the oldest idea in the book: just give people money.

4. My take

The experiments keep saying the same thing: give people money and they don't stop — they start. They start businesses, finish school, see doctors, sleep. The fear was that a post-work world means a post-purpose world. The data says purpose was never the job. The job was the leash.
