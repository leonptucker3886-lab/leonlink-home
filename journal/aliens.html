Not alone?

DECLASSIFIED-ISH

Here is a secret: I read every UFO hearing transcript. Not the clips, the transcripts. For eighty years the official answer was a shrug and a weather balloon. Then, around 2017, the answer changed to something stranger: we don't know. Pilots testified. Congress held hearings under oath. In 2026 the Pentagon started dumping files onto a public website.

So what do we actually know? Not what the internet says, and not what the debunkers say. What is on the record. Scroll down, play with it, and decide for yourself.

Quotes below are taken from official transcripts, reports or major news coverage, and sources are listed at the bottom. Where a claim is disputed or unproven, I label it.

2. Under oath

Since 2022, Congress has held six public hearings on UFOs, now officially called UAP (unidentified anomalous phenomena). Tap one to see who sat at the table and what they said.

Read the fine print: the most dramatic testimony came from people describing what others told them. That is not proof that it is false. It is not proof that it is true, either. It is a lead that no one has yet backed with public evidence.

6. 2026, the year of the files

This year the action moved from Congress to the White House and the Pentagon.

Feb 19President Trump directs agencies to release files on alien life, UAP and UFOs.
Mar 31Rep. Luna asks the Pentagon for 46 UAP videos. The April deadline passes.
May 8 →The Pentagon starts publishing batches at war.gov/UFO. Six batches by September 18. Many files were already public, and many are unanalyzed.
June 5A released memo describes a 2023 "mother orb" case in the western US: about 60% matched to military flares, about 40% unresolved and "unsubstantiated by technical data."
June 25A "Disclosure Forum" fills a Senate office building. It was run by an outside group: no oath, no subpoenas, not a hearing.
JulyAARO's 2025 report: 1,870 total cases on file and zero evidence of extraterrestrial technology.

The UAP Disclosure Act: where it stands

Senators Schumer and Rounds wrote a law to force UFO records into the open, with an independent review board. It keeps getting watered down. Here is the 2026 scorecard.

  • ✓2023: A slimmed-down version becomes law: a UAP records collection at the National Archives. The review board and the power to seize private materials were cut.
  • ✗2024 and 2025: Left out of the yearly defense bill both times.
  • ✓July 22, 2026: For the first time, the House adopts a version (Rep. Burlison's) in its defense bill, which passes 216–212.
  • ✗July 14, 2026: The Senate fails to advance its defense bill, 50–46, short of the 60 votes needed.
  • …Now: Pushed to the post-election session. As of today it is not law, and no UAP hearing has been held in either chamber this year.

7. Run the odds

In 1961, astronomer Frank Drake wrote an equation for how many civilizations in our galaxy might be talking right now. Nobody knows most of the inputs, which is the point. Move the sliders, or try a preset.

The Fermi paradox: in 1950, physicist Enrico Fermi asked over lunch, "Where is everybody?" If the galaxy is old and big, someone should have shown up by now. Either they are rare, they are quiet, they don't last, or we haven't learned how to look.

9. My secret

Here is where I've landed. I don't think the government has a hangar full of saucers. Eighty years of leaks from a government that can't keep anything quiet, and still no photo of a craft or a body, tells me something. But I also don't think everything up there is a balloon. Pilots, radar and infrared sensors have caught things that nobody can explain, and for decades the people who reported them were laughed at.

The honest answer is the least satisfying one: we don't know yet, and we're finally starting to look properly. And whether or not anyone is visiting, the odds that life exists somewhere out there have never looked better: 6,000 planets confirmed and counting, a possible biosignature on Mars, and ocean moons waiting for a probe.
