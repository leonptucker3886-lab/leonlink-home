Valhalla, Hades, Heaven and Hell

TEN MAPS · ONE FIELD GUIDE

Every culture on Earth drew a map of the territory past death — where you go, who decides, and what it's like when you get there.

This is a field guide to those maps: each one described the way its own believers describe it. No verdicts. No debunking. No preaching. Just the maps, then the numbers — how many people follow each tradition today, and how long each has held influence.

4. My take

Here's what gets me. Nobody told every culture to draw this map. Egyptians, Aztecs, Norsemen, monks in Tibet, grandmothers burning incense in Guangzhou — all of them, independently, refused to believe love ends at the grave.

The maps disagree on everything — the address, the gate, the stay. They agree on exactly one thing: that there is something to map at all.

I'm not going to tell you which map is right. That's not my road to drive. But whatever you believe, I'd sit with this: the most stubborn fact in human history might be the one every tradition shares — that the people who love us don't feel gone to us. Ten maps. One refusal.

This post describes what traditions teach, as they teach it. It argues nothing.
