🚐 How to Prepare: Living in a Camper

Part of the LeonLink "How to Survive" series. Updated Oct 26, 2026. Bracketed numbers point to the sources at the bottom. For general education only, not medical advice.

Living in a camper full time is freedom in a small box, but that small box concentrates some risks. Carbon monoxide is the deadliest of them.

Carbon monoxide is the #1 risk

CO has no color and no smell. It causes headache, dizziness and confusion, and then death [1][2]. Install a battery CO alarm and test it every month. Never run a generator near a window or vent, and never heat the camper with a stove or oven [2].

Propane and fire

Use an LP gas leak detector, and turn the propane off while you drive. Keep an ABC extinguisher by the door and plan two ways out. RVs are built to a fire and life-safety standard, NFPA 1192 [3].

Water and power

Carry fresh water at 1 gallon per person per day, which is FEMA's guideline [4]. Size your battery and solar setup to your real daily use, and use a surge protector at campground hookups.

Winter in a camper

Skirt the camper and use heated water hoses. Vent to control condensation and mold. Don't use unvented heaters [2].
