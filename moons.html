Ask the Col A new system invented here, with no old astrology or numerology

Col Got-ursix uses no human-made star formula. His Codex chains the days of the month into 12 War Signs (the same days in every month) and ties each one to Fire, Earth, Wind or Water. Your birth month raises a Banner, and your name crossed with your birthday forges your Star Rank, from 1 to 23. The same name and birthday always give the same result. How the Codex works

War Signs (day chains): days 1-3 Emberfang (Fire) · 4-6 Stonewarden (Earth) · 7-8 Galeborn (Wind) · 9-11 Tidecaller (Water) · 12-15 Ashcrown (Fire) · 16-17 Rootkeeper (Earth) · 18-19 Stormquill (Wind) · 20-21 Deepmire (Water) · 22-24 Flarewing (Fire) · 25-27 Ironvale (Earth) · 28-29 Skyrender (Wind) · 30-31 Moonwell (Water). For example, Jan 5 and Oct 5 are both Stonewarden.

Banner: your birth month raises one of 12 war banners, which shapes how your sign fights.

Star Rank: multiply each letter's place in the alphabet by its position in your name, then add them up to get the Name Weight. The Birth Weight is day × 31 + month × 12 + the last two digits of your birth year. Star Rank = ((Name Weight + Birth Weight + Age + 23) mod 23) + 1, so it always lands from 1 to 23. Because your age counts, your Star Rank shifts on every birthday.

Life Stage: your age sets one of 7 stages: Cadet (under 13), Recruit (13-19), Frontliner (20-29), Field Officer (30-44), Commander (45-59), Veteran (60-74) and Elder of the Stars (75+). Younger stages add Valor and Endurance; older stages add Wit, Fortune and Spirit.

Profile: every reading also returns your personality, pros, cons and best compatibility, drawn from your sign, banner, rank and life stage.

Capabilities: five scores from 0 to 100, set by your element, banner, life stage and Star Rank, then mixed with your name and birthday.

The Fated Days Almanac: Blessed & Cursed

Col Got-ursix keeps two kinds of marked days. War Holy Days fall on the same date every year, but the stars decide each year whether they come blessed or cursed. The Fated Day moves: every month has exactly one, and it lands on a different day and carries a different verdict every year.

How the verdict is cast: Omen = ((year mod 23)×7 + month² + day×3 + 23) mod 5. An Omen of 0, 1 or 2 means Blessed; 3 or 4 means Cursed. The monthly Fated Day = ((year×7 + month×23) mod 28) + 1, stepping one day forward if it lands on a Holy Day. The same year always gives the same almanac.

The Chronicle of the Ancient Wars

Before the stars had names, the Void Fleet swept across the sky and swallowed every light. Twelve soldiers, one for each chain of days, held the line. When the last battle ended, the Moon split their courage across the calendar so that every month would carry all twelve. That is why a child born on the 5th of any month is a Stonewarden, and why the 15th always burns with Ashcrown fire.

Col Got-ursix marched in all of those wars. He was a young signal keeper when Emberfang struck the first spark and an old officer when Moonwell wrote the last name in the Book of Stars. He has read the Codex for every soldier since, and he has never once used a chart drawn by human hands.

The Laws of the Codex: I. Your day sets your sign. II. Your month raises your banner. III. Your name and your years forge your rank. IV. The same soldier always gets the same reading. V. No reading is a sentence; every soldier can rise.

Emberfang. The first spark of the Ancient Wars. When the Void Fleet blotted out the stars, a lone scout named Emberfang struck flint on her shield and the whole army saw the way home.

Stonewarden. Stonewarden held the Pass of Kettle Moon for forty nights with nine soldiers. The rock itself is said to remember his footprints.

Galeborn. Born in the gale that tore the Sky Banner loose, Galeborn caught it mid-air and carried messages between twelve camps before dawn.

Tidecaller. Tidecaller could hear the enemy's plans in the rhythm of the sea. Every Water sign since carries a little of that listening.

Ashcrown. Ashcrown fell three times in the Siege of the Red Comet and rose three times. The crown of ash on her helm became the Fire signs' symbol of return.

Rootkeeper. Rootkeeper planted the first orchard on the scorched plain after the war. Soldiers of every sign still eat from its descendants.

Stormquill. Stormquill ended the Ninety-Day War with a single letter. Nobody knows what it said; every Wind sign claims to.

Deepmire. Deepmire mapped the tunnels beneath the Moon Wall that no enemy ever found. Her map was burned so the secret would live only in Water signs.

Flarewing. Flarewing flew the last ship through the Sun Breaker's fire, wings alight, and laughed the whole way.

Ironvale. Ironvale's forge never went cold during the Long Winter. Every blade carried by the Earth signs was tempered there.

Skyrender. Skyrender tore a hole in the storm wall so the stars could be seen again, and was court-martialled and knighted in the same week.

Moonwell. Moonwell tended the wounded of both sides after the final battle and wrote every soldier's name in the Book of Stars. Col Got-ursix keeps that book today.

Theories of the Codex (peer-reviewed by one very old colonel)

1. The Day-Chain Theory. Courage doesn't follow the seasons, it follows the calendar page. The Moon split the twelve soldiers across the days of the month, so every month runs a full war cycle: Fire opens, Earth holds, Wind scouts, Water heals, and then it repeats three times. That's why your sign comes from your day, not your month.

2. The Banner Drift Theory. Your month doesn't change who you are, it changes how you fight. An Emberfang under the Frozen March banner strikes just as hard as one under the Burning Field, only with better gloves.

3. The Rank Forge Theory. Your name is your weapon and your birthday is the anvil. Every year of life adds one hammer strike (that's the Age term), so your Star Rank is reforged on every birthday. The +23 honours the 23 ranks of the Cosmic Army, and mod 23 makes sure nobody, not even a General, outranks the stars.

4. The War-Wheel Harmony Theory. Fire fuels Wind, Wind carries Water, Water feeds Earth and Earth shelters Fire. Allies sit beside you on the wheel; your hardest match sits across it and owns exactly the skill you're missing. Fighting them is optional. Learning from them is not.

5. The Veteran's Paradox. Valor fades with age and Wit rises to replace it. The Codex calls this "getting smarter about which hills to die on." Elders of the Stars rarely charge; they send a Recruit and a strongly worded letter.

6. The Consistency Law. Same name, same birthday, same reading, every time. The Col has seen enough fortune tellers change their answer after a second coin. He considers it desertion.
