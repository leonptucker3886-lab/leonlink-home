The story

What is Leon Link?

One chimney sweep. One laptop. A grudge against overpriced software.

The short version

I run a small chimney servicing company. One truck, one person, real customers, real soot. For years I did what every small operator does: paid for the software the internet told me I needed.

And every year it got worse. The price climbed. The features I actually used stayed the same. The add-ons multiplied. The support got a script instead of a person. I was paying more to be treated worse, and every alternative on the market was the same product with a different logo.

So I stopped waiting for someone to fix it. I taught myself to build software and became a one-man developer — to cut through the noise, drop the bloat, and give working people something that costs what it should.

The best software for a small crew shouldn't cost more than the truck payment.

What I was up against

None of this is a secret. It's just priced like one.

Fees that grow with your ambition

Per-seat pricing means every new hire costs you before he swings a hammer. Your growth is their revenue model.

A hundred identical choices

Same features, same per-head math, same annual contract — a shelf of logos selling one product.

Support that evaporates

Once you've paid, you're routed to a script. Try reaching a human who can actually change something.

Prices built for someone else

Pricing aimed at companies with offices. The two-truck shop pays the same per head as the fifty-truck fleet and gets less.

So I became the developer

Leon Link is what happens when the customer gets a compiler. One person designs it, builds it, supports it, and answers the email. There is no sales team talking you into a tier you don't need, and no board pricing an add-on you didn't ask for.

Everything here runs on the same rules: one flat price, whole crew included, no per-user fees, no lock-in — and you can export your own data with one click, because a price you can't leave isn't a price.
