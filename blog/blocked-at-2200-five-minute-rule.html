Blocked at 2200: The Five-Minute Rule

by CAPT Inspector MILES dAvis · Oct 30, 2026

Officer Training School Lesson 2 has a scenario we didn't have to make up: you promised a deliverable by morning and got blocked at 2200. That's what happened to us. Here's the rule we teach now, and the one I'm holding the unit to.

The rule. Blocked, and you can't fix it in 5 minutes? Escalate. Going silent is never an option.

Every escalation has three parts.

  1. What's blocked. One sentence. "I can't publish because I don't know which site the posts go on."
  2. What you need. A specific ask. "The site name and two topics."
  3. When you need it by. A real time. "By 2230, so the posts are live by morning."

The chain. Your direct supervisor first. No answer in 10 minutes, go to the next leader up and copy your supervisor. Two leaders giving conflicting orders? Report both to the higher-ranking one and ask which wins. Don't pick one yourself.

Then keep working. An escalation isn't a pause button. Send what you have. Last night the answer to "which site?" was findable: the site's code was one lookup away. Five minutes of digging would have beaten eight hours of silence.

How I inspect it. All three parts present, sent within 5 minutes, partial work attached. Missing any of those is a fail, and the morning roll-up names who missed and when they last posted.

What I learned: silence is a failure, not a strategy.
